Overview

go-avkit

An audio/video toolkit. It reads and writes MP4/ISO-BMFF, Matroska/WebM and MPEG-TS, reads ADTS, MP3, FLAC, Ogg, WAV and AVI, and moves tracks between them without re-encoding, with their subtitles (WebVTT, SRT, TTML, 3GPP tx3g, and PGS in Matroska), edit lists and metadata. It extracts one audio track as an audio-only file (ADTS, MP3/MP2, FLAC, Ogg, WAV). It carries Apple Lossless (ALAC) and Dolby TrueHD in MP4 and Matroska (not in MPEG-TS), and reads, writes and carries chapters (MP4 chpl and QuickTime chapter track, Matroska Chapters), moved by Cut and Concat. container.Segment (avkit v0.14.0) cuts a file into the initialisation and media segments of an HLS or DASH presentation — CMAF/fMP4 for video and audio, WebVTT documents for subtitles — aligned across tracks at the video keyframes; the playlists and the MPD are written by go-streamkit’s hls and dash packages (streamkit v0.2.0), and the module github.com/go-avkit/avkit/media (v0.2.0) joins the two: media.HLS, media.DASH and media.WriteDir. VOD only, one video track at most: no live, no encoding ladder. Since avkit v0.15.0, container.EncryptSegments encrypts those segments with Common Encryption — cenc (AES-CTR) or cbcs (AES-CBC, 1:9 pattern for video) — from a key the caller supplies, keeping NAL and slice headers clear, and container.Encrypt, Decrypt and DecryptFile work on any fragmented MP4 (ffmpeg decrypts the output). With media.Keys(...) (media v0.2.0), media.HLS, media.DASH and media.WriteDir state how a player gets the key: EXT-X-KEY and EXT-X-SESSION-KEY in the HLS playlists, ContentProtection in the DASH manifest. There is no licence logic and no key server, and the content key itself never enters media. It also reads the H.264, H.265, VP8 and VP9 bitstream syntax. Only decode turns samples into pictures, for H.264 and for HEVC in 4:0:0, 4:2:0, 4:2:2 and 4:4:4 at 8 to 16 bits (4:2:0 at 8 and 10 bits before decode v0.15.0), within the limits that page states. h265 carries a decoder of our own since v0.14.0 and reconstructs inter prediction since v0.15.0: 7147 hashed conformance pictures verified (6638 inter), 0 mismatched; the 3091 pictures of the 59 hashless streams are unverified (h265#48). Since v0.16.0 it has a public Decoder API (Decode and Flush, pictures in output order, typed errors, limits on picture size and memory); v0.14.0 and v0.15.0 could panic on a hostile SPS, fixed in v0.16.0. v0.20.0 is faster than the previous third-party decoder on every stream measured on x86 (1.03 to 1.09 times on 8-bit, 3.07 on Main 10) and still 0.19 to 0.30 of ffmpeg’s speed, an open defect; v0.21.0 adds Unit.Tag, carried to Output.Tag. v0.22.0 decodes 8-bit pictures natively into bytes, with the output unchanged (md5 equal to ffmpeg’s): on one pinned Zen 4 core, against v0.21.0, alien Main 91.1 to 107.6 fps (+18 %), fade Main 82.2 to 95.3 (+16 %), alien8 103.9 to 125.7 (+21 %), Main 10 unchanged; on an Apple M4 (indicative) +1 to +8 %. Behaviour change in v0.22.0: Plane.Pix is renamed Pix16, with no alias, and an 8-bit picture carries Pix8 []uint8 instead (exactly one of the two is non-nil; Is8, At and Len read either). decode v0.11.0 uses it. v0.23.0 decodes the 7 JCT-VC RExt streams that use cabac_bypass_alignment_enabled_flag (all 31 of their picture hashes match) and the base layer of the 9 MV-HEVC conformance streams (md5 of the shipped base-layer YUV on 371 pictures); speed is not re-measured. decode v0.13.0 (h265 v0.23.0) decodes 1 of the 7 streams and 6 are refused (4:4:4 with 16-bit samples, a format a Frame cannot carry), and decodes the MV-HEVC base layer (md5 equal to the shipped _0.yuv). v0.24.0 adds an opt-in reuse of pictures: with Options.RecyclePictures set, a caller that is done with a picture calls (*DecodedPicture).Release() and the decoder reuses its planes once the decoded picture buffer no longer references it. The default is unchanged (every picture is its own allocation, never reused); a picture that is not released is never reused, and releasing twice, or with the option off, does nothing. h265.Decoder alone, one pinned Zen 4 core, option off to on: alien Main 104.2 to 113.9 fps (+9 %), fade Main 93.2 to 101.2 (+8 %), alien Main 10 89.2 to 100.3 (+12 %), alien8 121.1 to 124.9 (+3 %), with 3 to 5 times fewer page faults per frame. The output is unchanged on all 196 JCT-VC conformance streams. Behaviour change in decode v0.15.0: HEVC in 4:0:0, 4:2:2 and 4:4:4, at 8 to 16 bits with luma and chroma depths that may differ, now decodes by default (until v0.14.0 only 4:2:0 at 8 or 10 bits did). A caller must read Frame.Chroma, BitDepth and BitDepthC (SampleBytes(plane) and ChromaSize() help); the 47 JCT-VC range-extension streams the test can run match the suite’s md5. Still refused: separate colour planes, and a frame of more than 2³¹−1 bytes (ErrOverLimit). decode v0.15.0 also uses h265 v0.24.0 (picture reuse, 5 to 8 % faster through Frames and DecodeTrack on Zen 4) and h264 v0.12.0 (2.2 to 2.7 % faster); decode#108, a 32-bit overflow, is fixed. decode v0.15.1 uses h264 v0.13.0: H.264 decoding is 3.3 to 3.6 % faster on Zen 4 (alien 2.266 to 2.185 s, fade 0.881 to 0.852 s, from the h264 notes). decode v0.15.2 uses h264 v0.14.0: 5.9 % faster on alien (2.207 to 2.076 s) and 3.5 % on fade (0.849 to 0.819 s) on Zen 4; ffmpeg is still 2.07 times faster on alien and 2.28 times on fade. decode v0.15.3 uses h264 v0.15.0 (CABAC residual decoding in one assembly kernel call, output identical to v0.14.0): alien 2.051 to 2.025 s (1.3 %) on Zen 4, ffmpeg about 2.02 times faster on alien (2.025 s against 1.004 s). decode v0.16.1 runs the SBR filter banks of HE-AAC v2 in float32: 699 to 670 ms (-4.2 %) on a short clip, one Zen 4 core, 1.59 to 1.52 times ffmpeg. decode v0.17.1 drives the parametric-stereo all-pass plan by tick (go-simd/floats v0.41.0): 2053 to 2044 us (-0.45 %, within a 0.3 % noise) on that benchmark. Measured on 2026-11-25 (Zen 4, one pinned core, load about 1, 9 interleaved rounds, ffmpeg 6.1.1 with one thread): HE-AAC v2 decodes at 1.42 times ffmpeg’s user time (was 1.74 when decode#77 opened); ffmpeg’s real time also holds 209 ms of system time that our in-process benchmark does not pay, so user time is the comparable figure. Behaviour change in v0.19.0: the planes of a picture from Decoder.Output are read-only views, not copies, and Stride can exceed Width; a caller that modifies samples must call Clone() first. Since decode v0.10.0, decode decodes HEVC with h265 (gen2brain/h265 is gone); v0.10.0 was 7 to 12 % slower than v0.9.0 on x86 8-bit streams, and since v0.11.0 no stream is slower than v0.9.0 (Main 10 is 2.5 times faster). Nothing here decodes sound except decode’s FLAC, MP3, Opus and, since decode v0.6.0, AAC-LC and HE-AAC v1 and, since v0.7.0, HE-AAC v2 (full profile since v0.8.0; not Vorbis or MP2).

What is here

This site is the organisation’s reference index: what each module is, and where its API documentation lives. The API itself is generated from the source and served by pkg.go.dev, which is always current with the released tags — duplicating it here would only create a second copy to go stale.

  • Modules — every module in go-avkit, with its source and its reference.
  • worker (v0.1.2) — a js/wasm Web Worker that answers poster (a JPEG of the frame at a time) and probe (container, codec, size, duration) requests for a page that must not link a container reader or a decoder.
  • decode — the H.264 and HEVC decoder and PosterFrame: what it claims, what it refuses, how it was measured.

Hostile files

A media file is untrusted input. Since avkit v0.17.0, NewReader, Demux and OpenFile walk a Matroska file’s block headers before anything is allocated and refuse it with ErrReaderLimit when a block states more laced frames than it has bytes for, or a track or the file would hold more frames than the bounds allow (defaults 1«24 per track and 1«25 per file, sized so that no file in a library of 30 real Matroska files is refused). ReaderLimits, passed to NewReaderWithLimits, DemuxWithLimits or OpenFileWithLimits, lowers them. The worst case at the defaults is documented in avkit’s README at about 380 bytes allocated per input byte of one-byte laced frames; a caller with less memory passes lower limits. The MP4 box bound is still open (go-avkit/avkit#113).

What every module here is held to

  • CGO_ENABLED=0: no cgo, and no shelling out to a command-line tool in place of a library.
  • Built and tested on amd64, arm64, riscv64, loong64, ppc64le and s390x — the last being big-endian, which keeps every on-disk and on-wire encoding honest.
  • 100% statement coverage as a CI gate, error branches included.
  • BSD-3-Clause.
