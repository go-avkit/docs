decode

decode

github.com/go-avkit/decode decodes H.264 and HEVC / H.265 (4:0:0, 4:2:0, 4:2:2 and 4:4:4 at 8 to 16 bits since v0.15.0; 4:2:0 at 8 and 10 bits before) to planar YUV and FLAC, MP3, Opus, since v0.6.0, AAC and, since v0.9.0, linear PCM (WAVE) to PCM, in pure Go with no cgo. Vorbis and MP2 are not decoded. H.264 is decoded by oops1/go.264 (BSD-2-Clause), pinned and forced onto its software path; HEVC, since v0.10.0, by our own h265 v0.22.0 since v0.11.0 (it was gen2brain/h265 up to v0.9.0; that dependency is gone; until v0.14.0 a format a Frame could not carry was refused at the SPS with an ErrUnsupported that names it, and since v0.15.0 only separate colour planes are). Speed is on the home page: v0.10.0’s x86 8-bit slowdown (decode#101) is closed, and no stream is slower than v0.9.0 since v0.11.0. decode is the avkit-facing layer around them and the place where claims are measured. It is the only module in this organisation that produces pictures or sound.

Using it 

r, _ := container.NewReader(file)
frames, err := decode.DecodeTrack(r, trackID, decode.Config{})
for _, f := range frames { // display order
    use(f.Y, f.Cb, f.Cr, f.YStride, f.CStride, f.Width, f.Height, f.PTS)
}

Since v0.14.0, decode.Frames is the streaming form of DecodeTrack, for H.264 and HEVC, with the same edit-list and timestamp handling:

for f, err := range decode.Frames(r, trackID, decode.Config{}) {
    if err != nil {
        return err
    }
    use(f)
}

It returns an iter.Seq2[*Frame, error]. Each *Frame is a fresh allocation the caller owns, so it can be kept or sent to another goroutine; leaving the loop (break, return) stops the decoding; an error is the last pair, after every picture decoded before it. On the 15 files of the edit-list corpus it yields exactly DecodeTrack’s pictures, and both equal ffmpeg’s md5. A caller that uses each frame and lets it go saves the slice: on Zen 4, one core, Frames against DecodeTrack, H.264 alien 2.63 s to 2.53 s (-4 %) with page faults 194 000 to 25 000, fade 1.05 s to 1.00 s (-5 %), HEVC alien-main 2.66 s to 2.47 s (-7 %).

Since v0.15.0, decode uses h265 v0.24.0 and releases each picture to it once it is converted to a Frame, so the decoder reuses the planes; the output is unchanged. Zen 4, one core, 7 ABBA rounds, median of 14 runs per side, against v0.14.0 (this measures that change alone): alien-main Frames 2.401 s to 2.213 s (-7.8 %), DecodeTrack 2.580 s to 2.444 s (-5.3 %); fade-main Frames 1.358 s to 1.261 s (-7.2 %), DecodeTrack 1.461 s to 1.386 s (-5.1 %); minor page faults per frame on alien-main Frames 1437 to 283. H.264 runs on h264 v0.12.0 (a store-forwarding stall removed), 2.2 to 2.7 % faster on Zen 4: alien 2.561 s to 2.491 s, fade 1.034 s to 1.011 s. Since v0.15.1, decode uses h264 v0.13.0: H.264 decoding is 3.3 to 3.6 % faster on Zen 4 (alien 2.266 s to 2.185 s, fade 0.881 s to 0.852 s, from the h264 notes). Since v0.15.2, it uses h264 v0.14.0: 5.9 % faster on alien (2.207 s to 2.076 s) and 3.5 % on fade (0.849 s to 0.819 s) on Zen 4; ffmpeg 6.1.1 is still faster on H.264, 2.07 times on alien and 2.28 times on fade. Since v0.15.3, it uses h264 v0.15.0 (CABAC residual decoding in one assembly kernel call, output identical to v0.14.0 under a differential fuzz test): alien 2.051 s to 2.025 s (1.3 %) on Zen 4; ffmpeg is about 2.02 times faster on alien (2.025 s against 1.004 s).

  • H.264 input: an avcC record (decode.NewAVCC) or a TrackConfig (decode.NewTrack) with length-prefixed samples, fed with DecodeSample and ended with Flush; or a raw Annex B stream (decode.New, DecodeAnnexB).
  • HEVC input: the same calls choose HEVC for a track whose sample entry is hvc1 or hev1; decode.NewHVCC takes an hvcC record, decode.NewHEVC takes in-band parameter sets, and DecodeAnnexB a raw .265 stream. Main 10 frames hold little-endian 16-bit samples.
  • Output: frames in display order, with planes, strides, size, chroma format, bit depth and PTS. Behaviour change in v0.15.0: a Frame may now be 4:0:0, 4:2:0, 4:2:2 or 4:4:4, at 8 to 16 bits, with luma and chroma depths that differ. Read Frame.Chroma, BitDepth and BitDepthC rather than assuming 4:2:0: (Frame).SampleBytes(plane) gives the bytes per sample of a plane (1 at 8 bits, otherwise 2, a little-endian 16-bit word) and (Frame).ChromaSize() the size of the chroma planes. A 4:0:0 frame has no Cb or Cr, and its BitDepthC is 0. Frames are bounded by their bytes, computed without overflow, so the same limit applies on every platform (decode#108, a 32-bit overflow, is fixed).

One picture 

id, _ := decode.FirstVideoTrack(r)
f, err := decode.PosterFrame(r, id, 12.5, decode.PosterConfig{})

PosterFrame (since decode v0.2.0) returns the picture with the greatest presentation time at or before t, the very bytes DecodeTrack returns for it, without decoding the whole track: decoding starts at the last sync sample at or before the picture’s sample and stops as soon as the picture is out. It takes H.264 (avc1, avc3) and HEVC (hvc1, hev1); another codec (VP8, VP9, AV1, …) is refused by name, since v0.9.0, with a *CodecError (codec "vp09" is not supported; errors.Is(err, ErrUnsupported) holds), not reported as a corrupt sample. A negative or NaN t is ErrTime.

Edit lists

Since v0.5.0, DecodeTrack and PosterFrame apply an MP4 track’s edit list by default, as ffmpeg does: a picture presented before an edit’s media time, or at its end or after, is decoded but not returned, presentation times are shifted so the pictures of the edit start at 0 (or at the length of the empty edit that delays the track), and PosterFrame’s t is a presentation time. This is a change from v0.3.0 for a file whose edit list trims or delays the track. Config.IgnoreEditList (ffmpeg’s -ignore_editlist 1) returns every picture labelled with track time, as v0.3.0 did.

An edit list the package does not apply is refused with ErrEditList rather than guessed at (a media rate other than 1, an edit lasting to the end of the media, an empty edit after the first edit that shows media, a media time before the track). Matroska has no per-track video edit list, so the first picture’s time is now 0, as in ffmpeg. Known divergence: for a fragmented MP4 with a trimmed start, ffmpeg and decode return different pictures (21 against 19 on the fixture), because ffmpeg switches its edit list support off for a fragmented file and decode cannot tell the file is fragmented yet (go-avkit/decode#20).

Audio 

id, _ := decode.FirstAudioTrack(r)
pcm, err := decode.DecodeAudioTrack(r, id, decode.AudioConfig{})
for _, a := range pcm { // presentation order, trimmed as the container states
    use(a.Data, a.Format, a.SampleRate, a.Channels, a.Layout, a.PTS)
}

NewAudioTrack streams it with DecodeSample and Flush. Data is interleaved little-endian PCM (s16le, s32le or f32le). The start of the track is dropped as its container states it (edit list, Opus pre-skip).

Linear PCM takes its width and byte order from the pcmC record of the track’s sample entry; an entry without the record is ErrAudioConfig.

  • FLAC: 4 to 24 bits, 1 to 8 channels; a 32-bit stream is refused with ErrAudioUnsupported.
  • MP3: MPEG-1, 2 and 2.5, mono and stereo. MP2 (Layer II) is not decoded and is refused with ErrAudioUnsupported.
  • Opus: mono and stereo (mapping family 0); more channels (for example 5.1) are refused with ErrAudioUnsupported. Opus is checked against the RFC 8251 conformance vectors, not against ffmpeg’s output sample for sample.
  • Vorbis is not decoded: the one pure-Go decoder tried failed the comparison with ffmpeg, and no decoder was written.

AAC (since v0.6.0)

AAC is available since decode v0.6.0; v0.5.4 and earlier do not decode it. HE-AAC v2 follows in v0.7.0, with its full profile by default in v0.8.0.

  • AAC-LC, written in Go from ISO/IEC 14496-3 (mono to 7.1, 8 to 96 kHz; M/S and intensity stereo, perceptual noise substitution, TNS, pulses, short windows), output f32le. Main, LTP, SSR, 960-sample frames, coupling channels and channel configurations 11, 12 and 14 are refused with ErrAudioUnsupported.
  • HE-AAC v1 (SBR over AAC-LC) decodes at twice the core rate, whether the AudioSpecificConfig signals it or the stream only carries it. SBR in its downsampled mode is refused.
  • Gapless: an MP4’s iTunSMPB item and edit lists are applied to AAC as ffmpeg does. DecodeAudioTrack applies it, NewAudioTrack does not.
  • HE-AAC v2 (Parametric Stereo) is supported since v0.7.0, and the full profile is the default since v0.8.0: procedure B for ICC modes 3 to 5, and IPD/OPD with their smoothing and interpolation, as the _ur references of ISO’s vectors are. 34-band streams keep 34 bands (a baseline decoder maps them to 20, so one ISO vector, al_sbr_ps_06, matches its _ur reference but not its baseline one, 2226 LSB off). v0.7.0 mixed with baseline mixing only (procedure A, IPD/OPD ignored). A mono core that signals SBR is announced as stereo from the first frame. Checked: all 7 of 7 ISO PS vectors match the _ur references at 0.033 LSB, and 801 mono PS files of the author’s library are within 1.1e-5 of ffmpeg 9.0.2.
  • Checked: the error against a float64 reference of the same bitstream is about half of ffmpeg’s on the five fixtures; HE-AAC is within 3e-6 of ffmpeg 9.0.2 on four; 12 of the 15 ISO/IEC 14496-26 conformance vectors pass.

Speed (go-avkit/decode#35): a decode-only benchmark (BenchmarkAACDecodeReuse, one core) against the median utime of ffmpeg 9.0.2 -threads 1, which is a whole process that also opens and demuxes the file. Go 1.27.4, Apple M4 Max, 10-minute inputs, five interleaved rounds at 1-minute load 6.02 to 6.46:

How it was measured

Every picture’s MD5 is compared with ffmpeg’s -f framemd5, single thread.

  • ITU-T H.264.1 conformance suite (AVCv1): 81 streams tried, 73 give the same pictures as ffmpeg. 69 do with go.264 v1.12.0 as pinned; 4 more (MR2 to MR5_TANDBERG) only with the upstream fix oops1/go.264#2, which is merged upstream and not yet in a release. The other 8 are not claimed: 5 interlaced and 3 using FMO/ASO.
  • JCT-VC conformance suite HEVC_v1 (ITU-T H.265.1): 147 streams. 143 give the same pictures as ffmpeg. Three are decided by the suite’s own MD5 because ffmpeg disagrees with it and decode agrees with it (SAODBLK_A_MainConcept_4, SAODBLK_B_MainConcept_4, VPSSPSPPS_A_MainConcept_1). One, TSUNEQBD_A_MAIN10_Technicolor_2 (luma and chroma bit depths differ), is refused by ffmpeg; since v0.15.0 decode decodes it to the suite’s MD5. The bitstreams are not in the repository, so the conformance test runs only when pointed at them.
  • JCT-VC range extensions (since v0.15.0): all 47 streams the test can run decode to the suite’s md5, including Bitdepth_A, Bitdepth_B and TSUNEQBD (10-bit luma, 9-bit chroma).
  • Streams and real files in the repository: 29 H.264 and 5 HEVC streams run in CI against ffmpeg’s MD5s; real MP4 and Matroska files (5 H.264, 7 HEVC) are recorded as hashes.

Limits

A stream is untrusted. Config.MaxFrameMBs (default 36864, a 4096x2304 picture) and MaxSampleBytes (default 64 MiB) are checked before anything sized by the stream is allocated. Bomb tests assert a refusal under a memory limit for both video codecs, and the parsers and sample decoders are fuzzed with a committed corpus. AudioConfig limits the channel count, the block size, a coded sample and the PCM a track may return. PosterFrame has bounds of its own in PosterConfig: MaxSamples (default 600) caps the samples decoded for one picture, past it the error is ErrPosterLimit, and MaxTrackSamples (default 1«20) refuses a longer sample list.

Speed

decode is slower than ffmpeg on every video input measured, and that is an open defect (go-avkit/decode#2). Measured on decode v0.3.0, single core, on an Apple M4 Max with ffmpeg 9.0.2 -threads 1, 5 interleaved rounds:

Inputdecode (fps)ffmpeg (fps)decode / ffmpeg
H.264 High 1080p, real clip36.5173.10.21
HEVC Main 10 2160p, real clip10.327.90.37
committed H.264 and HEVC streams0.21 to 0.41

This table is the v0.3.0 measurement, when HEVC ran on gen2brain/h265; the HEVC speed since v0.10.0 is on the home page. The profiles put the hot spots mostly in the wrapped libraries: go.264 has no arm64 assembly kernels, and gen2brain/h265 took its assembly motion compensation at 8 bits only. The measurement is on arm64; an amd64 one is still needed. The audio decoders are not measured yet.
